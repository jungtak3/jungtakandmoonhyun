## Direction contract

**THESIS:** The invitation is an envelope, not a web page. The guest reads paper that has just been opened. This refuses the category default — a full-bleed hero photo, a centred love story, a scroll of stacked cards on cream.

**OWN-WORLD:** Ground is the inside of an opened envelope: deep plum `#221a1e`, warmed, not cold. Paper is `#fbfaf7`, printed in `#241c20` with `#6b5f63` for secondary and one wax-red `#a83a24` for the seal and emphasis. Paper is lit from behind — it carries a warm bloom rather than sitting flat. Rules are engraver's hairlines; squares are cut into the paper as a lattice, and an active one fills solid. State is drawn as a line's continuity, never as hue: an unbroken line is live, a gap is sealed.

**STORY:** A guest opens a KakaoTalk link and lands on an already-opened invitation: two names, a date, a place. Within one screen they know it is a real wedding and that they are invited. They scroll once to learn how to arrive, and they can reply. Nothing asks them to perform a ritual before they are allowed to read.

**FIRST VIEWPORT:** No gate. The envelope is presented open: its flap is folded back at the top of the viewport, drawn in paper-tone hairlines on the plum ground, and the invitation sheet is already rising out of it, filling the frame. On that sheet, in engraved serif: `초대합니다` set small and letterspaced above, then `정탁` and `문현` at display scale with the wax seal between them, then the date line and the venue, and a ruled row of the page's own navigation. The primary action is `참석 여부 전하기`, plainly visible without scrolling.

**FORM:** The page is one envelope seen once, from the front, already open. It never becomes two envelopes, never splits into panels, never gains a second card beside it. Everything lives on the one sheet that rose out of it, typeset like a printed programme: ruled columns, tabular figures, footnote-sized directions. The wax seal appears exactly twice — once as the mark between the names, once as the seal on the reply.

**SIGNATURE:** The sheet's light. The invitation sheet carries a soft warm bloom that deepens as it scrolls and settles into a printed page; the seal's fracture line is drawn, not photographed. On `prefers-reduced-motion` the page is simply the finished sheet.

**RISK:** The envelope interior is dark, which is unusual for a wedding and could read as sombre; the sheet's size, warmth, and the immediate presence of both names must counter it in the first screen. A metaphor that only works once must not thin out — everything below the fold has to earn its place as good printed matter.
